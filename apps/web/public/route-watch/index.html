Know when your regular route is having a bad day.

Route Watch monitors a saved Home → Work or Work → Home corridor against official NZTA Traffic and Travel road events. It is designed for the route you actually repeat, not wherever your phone happened to be when you switched monitoring on.

A commute watch should stay attached to the commute

A common failure mode for route monitoring is anchoring the watch to the phone's location at the exact moment the feature is enabled. That turns a regular commute into a moving target. Waybi instead stores a sampled route corridor between configured Home and Work places, together with baseline distance, ETA and the route provider.

Home → Work and Work → Home are independent watches. That matters because the two directions can use different roads, encounter different incidents and behave differently at different times of day.

Background checks use the saved corridor

The Waybi backend evaluates active, non-expired route watches every 15 minutes against official NZTA Traffic and Travel road events. The background process works from the saved corridor and does not need to continuously upload the driver's live GPS position.

Route Watch is not continuous location tracking. The monitoring job checks the route geometry saved for the watch. Live GPS remains a navigation-time input on the device rather than a requirement for the scheduled background check.

Route geometry is allowed to expire

A route is not permanent. Road networks, provider responses and a driver's preferred path can change, so Route Watch does not keep treating old geometry as authoritative forever. Saved geometry expires after 29 days and must be refreshed before monitoring can continue normally.

Waybi normalises watch state into practical categories such as healthy, advisory, warning, disrupted and unknown. An unknown state can be used when the route itself needs refreshing rather than pretending the corridor is definitely clear.

Why not buy a fresh route every 15 minutes?

Repeatedly requesting a paid provider route for every watch would create unnecessary API cost and can still make the monitored corridor drift. Waybi uses the stored route geometry for background event matching. Fresh route planning can happen when the user actually refreshes or uses the route, while periodic monitoring stays lightweight and predictable.

What Route Watch can say today

The current implementation provides real server-side monitoring and in-app route-watch status. Remote push delivery is a separate layer and is not presented as complete until APNs or FCM delivery is wired end to end. That distinction keeps the product honest: monitoring can be real without claiming a notification channel that is not yet finished.

Route Watch is a Plus feature

Waybi keeps basic navigation outside the subscription wall. Core maps, search, route planning, turn-by-turn navigation, automatic fixed-camera data refresh, Journey Brief, parking discovery and core trip history are designed to remain available without Plus. Route Watch belongs in Plus because it performs recurring proactive monitoring on behalf of the driver.

Route Watch questions

Does Route Watch track my live location all day?

No. Scheduled background checks use the stored route corridor. Waybi does not need a continuous live GPS stream to compare that corridor with road events.

How often is a route watch checked?

The production schedule evaluates active, non-expired route watches every 15 minutes against official NZTA road events.

Will Route Watch always send a push notification?

Not yet. The current implementation has real server-side monitoring and in-app status. Remote push delivery remains a separate integration and is not claimed as complete.

Why does a watch expire?

Saved route geometry expires after 29 days so Waybi does not silently monitor an increasingly stale corridor forever.
